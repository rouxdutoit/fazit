Enough open source exists. Fazit helps you reach it.

I’m Ruan “Roux” du Toit. I build Fazit in Feldkirchen, near Munich. I started in November 2026, four days after reading Jordan Krueger’s post. I want what I build to be for all of us. That is why Fazit is copyleft: anyone may use it, change it and share it, and it stays open.

Where it started

In my consulting work, companies have asked me whether they could go open source, and what it would take. There was no quick, honest way to answer.

Then I read a post by Jordan Krueger, and it clicked: we do not need more apps, we need better open source. Krueger asked people who build with AI to stop adding new things and help the tools others already rely on.

I had built projects because agents made them easy to build, not because anyone had asked for them. The post said what I already knew. This time, companies had asked.

Fazit is a new tool, so it does not follow that advice to the letter. It replaces nothing. openDesk, Nextcloud and LibreOffice already exist. What was missing was a way for an organisation to see, in its own numbers, what moving to them would take.

Public bodies and companies consider leaving Microsoft 365 for reasons such as digital sovereignty, data protection or cost. Before deciding, they need to know which functions they rely on. An assigned Teams Phone licence, for example, can reveal a dependency that has yet to make it into the migration plan.

Fazit shows the evidence for each finding and marks missing evidence as “Unknown”. It gives no overall score and recommends no supplier.

Why it is free

The organisations that most need these numbers are public bodies. The public pays for their software, so the public should be able to read the tool that measures it.

Fazit is free and open source under AGPL-3.0-or-later. All features and rules are in the repository. Anyone may use, change and share it. Anyone who passes on a changed version shares the changes too.

I believe machines should serve all of us. This is my part of that.

What you can check

Each of these is a statement you can test, not a promise to take on trust.

  • Open

    All code and rules are public under the AGPL-3.0-or-later licence.

  • Local

    Today’s version runs on your computer and contains no network code.

  • Unknown stays unknown

    The report shows a missing figure as missing, with the reason and a next step.

  • No tracking

    This site sets no cookies and loads nothing from other servers.

  • Not finished

    Fazit runs on sample data today. Import of real exports and a first release come next.

How it is made

I design Fazit: what it does, how it is structured and what counts as correct. I answer for what ships.

The AI tools Claude and Codex write the code. I work with them as I would with a team. We settle the architecture together, they write and test, and before each release other AI models read the whole codebase cold and look for faults.

I do not write the code and I do not read every line. I check the review findings, which are public.

Before each release, an open model running on my own computer will also review the code, so anyone can repeat the check offline with public weights.

The mappings to openDesk and Nextcloud need a person who runs them, not a model. That review has not happened yet.

How it pays its way

Fazit stays free. I plan to earn from assessments and support around it, through my consultancy at rouxdutoit.com. The first three pilot assessments are free.

How to help

Fazit’s mappings record where openDesk and Nextcloud cover a Microsoft 365 function and where they fall short. If you run either, read a mapping and correct what is wrong.

When Fazit shows a gap in an open source project, the fix belongs in that project, with the people who maintain it.
