# Fazit release review 1 (reviewer: Claude Fable 5.2)

Commit `82d9d8f`, checked in the private clone on 2026-11-22. The public `main` kept moving while I worked (`cf082d1` when I finished). Those later commits are not reviewed, and some may already touch findings below, F-10 for one.

## Verdict

**Release after fixes.** The tool itself held up under everything I threw at it, but four things around it would hurt on announcement day: the domain and PyPI name are not secured, a published principle is false, the site's own headers break its sample report, and the copy claims more than the tool can do today.

Findings: 0 critical, 4 high, 12 medium, 5 low.

## What I ran

`uv sync`, `pytest -q` (586 passed, 57 s), `ruff check`, `ruff format --check`, `site/check_site.py` (all clean). Both demos, all 12 sample × profile × language combinations, the README commands. 3,548 mutated or malformed `scan.json` files and 1,500 `inputs.yaml` files through `fazit report`. Path probes for `--out` (file, symlink, dangling link, directory, read-only, hard link, `/dev/zero`, loops). The demo and the installed-package tests inside a network namespace with no interfaces. Eight hash seeds. A local server applying `site/_headers`, loaded in headless Chromium 151. `uv build` and both archives. HTTP status of all 38 external URLs cited in rules, site and docs, and the text of six cited documentation pages. RDAP, PyPI and GitHub API lookups (private vulnerability reporting is enabled, CI is green). Town totals recomputed with exact fractions from `demo/town/inputs.yaml`.

Not verified: anything on Windows or macOS, and the deployed site, which does not exist yet.

## What is good and must not be lost

- **Input handling.** All 5,048 malformed files ended in one localised sentence and exit code 2, with positions instead of keys that could be private. No traceback, no hang.
- **A conservative engine.** In every variant I built, partial, unknown or skipped evidence never produced Ready, while a positive lower bound still confirmed a problem. The cost totals match my recomputation: 600,000, 527,800 and 464,350.
- **Privacy by construction.** Codes and counts only, unregistered licence identifiers anonymised, at signs rejected, markup escaped, `default-src 'none'`, and a test that plants names in every position of a scan.
- **Offline and deterministic.** The demo runs with no network interface, output is byte-identical across hash seeds and to the committed site demo, and writes are atomic and refuse links.
- **Honest sourcing.** Action SHAs match their tags, the lock is checked against the cutoff date, all 38 cited URLs resolve, the six mapping citations I opened support their notes, and the report states its own trust limits plainly.

## Ideas

1. **A report without a scan, today.** `fazit report --inputs inputs.yaml` alone. A hand-written empty non-sample scan already renders an interview-and-costs report; I tried it. It gives IT leads something real on day one and makes F-04 true.
2. **A one-page summary at the top**, written to be pasted into a council paper: statuses, open questions, three totals. That is the page decision-makers read.
3. **Generate the product rosters** in `service_plans.yaml` from Microsoft's published licensing CSV and maintain only the classification by hand, including free and education products. Otherwise almost every real tenant will hit "licence not classified" and six areas turn Unknown.
4. **Cumulative cost by year and a break-even month** from the existing inputs, plus an optional yearly price change supplied by the user. Flat Microsoft prices for five years is the first objection finance will raise.
5. **openCode.de and REUSE compliance.** German municipal IT providers look there first, and `publiccode.yml` is already written.
